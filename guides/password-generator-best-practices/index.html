Password generator best practices

A password generator is most useful when you combine randomness, enough length, and safe storage.

A password generator helps remove human patterns. People tend to choose words, dates, and substitutions that feel clever but are easy to guess. Random generation makes the result less predictable.

Use the Password Generator when creating a new account or replacing an old password. Choose a length that the site accepts and include a mix of character types when possible.

Recommended settings

  • Use at least 14 to 16 characters for normal accounts.
  • Use more characters for important accounts.
  • Include uppercase, lowercase, numbers, and symbols if the site supports them.
  • Avoid ambiguous characters only when you need to type the password manually.

Some websites have outdated limits or strange rules. If a site rejects a strong generated password, create another version that fits its rules while keeping as much length as possible.

Store it safely

Do not save generated passwords in plain text notes or screenshots. A password manager is designed to store them securely and fill them when needed. It also helps you avoid reusing the same password.

What a generator cannot fix

A strong password does not protect you from every risk. Phishing pages can still trick you into entering it. Malware can still steal data from a compromised device. Use two-factor authentication, keep recovery email secure, and watch for suspicious login alerts.

Random passwords are one layer. Good account habits complete the system.
