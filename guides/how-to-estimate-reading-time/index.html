How to estimate reading time

Reading time gives visitors a quick sense of commitment before they start reading.

Reading time is usually estimated by dividing the number of words by an average reading speed. A common default is about 200 to 250 words per minute for general web content. The result is an estimate, not a promise.

The Reading Time Calculator uses pasted text to estimate how long a reader may need. It is useful for blog posts, guides, newsletters, scripts, and documentation.

Why reading time helps

A visitor deciding whether to read now or later wants to know the size of the task. A two minute guide feels different from a fifteen minute report. Showing reading time sets expectations and can reduce bounce from surprise.

What affects reading speed

  • Technical vocabulary slows readers down.
  • Short paragraphs are easier to scan.
  • Tables, code, and charts take extra time.
  • Readers may skim instead of reading every word.

Because of these differences, round the estimate. Saying "about 4 minutes" is better than pretending the exact time is 3 minutes and 42 seconds.

How to improve a long page

If the reading time is higher than expected, break the page into sections. Add clear headings, remove repeated points, and move supporting details lower on the page. A longer page can still work when it is organized well.

Reading time is a small signal that makes a page feel more respectful of the visitor's attention.
